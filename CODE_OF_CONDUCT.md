# Code of conduct

## The short version

Everyone taking part in this project is an equal. Assume good faith. Argue about the
code, never about the person who wrote it.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct and be kind. Both at once is possible.
- Accept that people arrive with different experience, languages, time zones, and
  hardware. A question that looks obvious to you was not obvious to whoever asked it.
- Aim feedback at the work. "This function leaks a file handle" is useful. "You are
  careless" is not.
- Take correction plainly. Being wrong in public is an ordinary part of engineering.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of a discussion.
- Insults or attacks based on who somebody is: their nationality, ethnicity, gender,
  sexuality, disability, religion, age, or appearance.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Publishing somebody's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately contributing malicious code, or misrepresenting what a change does.

## Scope

This applies everywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, commit messages,
code review, discussions, and any private correspondence that follows from them.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainer, [@aichholzer](https://github.com/aichholzer), on
GitHub. Say what happened, where, and when. A link is usually enough.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone and handled in confidence. A good-faith
report will not be held against the person who makes it, whatever the outcome.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides the response. The steps run from lightest to heaviest:

1. A private word about the behaviour.
2. A public correction on the thread.
3. A temporary block from participating.
4. A permanent block.

A serious incident goes straight to the later steps.
